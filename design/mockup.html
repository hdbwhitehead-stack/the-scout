What these bets actually are

Each row is a short-tail-risk position. You collect a small premium in exchange for accepting a small probability of a large, total loss. The bet looks boring most of the time. That’s the point. Over a large enough basket, the premiums compound; the occasional blow-up is the cost of doing business.

The APR figure, with caveats

APR is computed as (1 − price) ÷ price ÷ years_to_resolution, assuming the position is held to resolution. Early exit is possible but costs the bid-ask spread, which on Polymarket is often wider than the premium itself for thinly-traded markets. Watch the liquidity column.

What the risk score does and does not mean

The risk score (1–5) is an LLM judge’s read on the resolution criterion, not the market. A 5 means the question is ambiguous, depends on subjective adjudication, or could plausibly never resolve. A 1 means a clean, oracle-friendly question with a public-record answer. The score says nothing about whether the favored side will actually win.

Disclaimer, in plain English

This is a research toy. It is not financial advice. Prediction markets are illegal in many jurisdictions and unregulated everywhere else. Do not bet money you cannot afford to lose. Do not bet money you can afford to lose, either, if you have not read the resolution rules in full. The author is, at best, a hobbyist.
