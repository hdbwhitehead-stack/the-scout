What these bets actually are

Each row is a short-tail-risk position. You collect a small premium in exchange for accepting a small probability of a large, total loss. The bet looks boring most of the time. That’s the point. Over a large enough basket, the premiums compound; the occasional blow-up is the cost of doing business.

The YTM figure, with caveats

YTM (yield to maturity) is computed as (1 − price) ÷ price ÷ years_to_resolution, assuming the position is held to resolution. Early exit is possible but costs the bid-ask spread, which on Polymarket is often wider than the premium itself for thinly-traded markets. Watch the liquidity column.

What the risk score does and does not mean

The risk score (1–5) is an LLM judge’s read on the resolution criterion, not the market. A 5 means the question is ambiguous, depends on subjective adjudication, or could plausibly never resolve. A 1 means a clean, oracle-friendly question with a public-record answer. The score says nothing about whether the favored side will actually win.

The risk-based probability adjustment

Resolution-risk haircut: subjective P(win) is reduced before computing edge — 0pp for risk_score 1–2, 5pp for risk 3, 10pp for risk 4, 20pp for risk 5. This is an explicit policy adjustment, not the LLM’s view. The LLM’s raw estimate is shown in the detail row alongside the adjusted figure used for sizing.

How the Size column is computed

Suggested size is min(0.25 × Kelly, 1% of bankroll). Full Kelly on these bets is brutal — loss-on-wrong is often 15–20× win-on-right — so quarter-Kelly is the prudent floor and a hard 1% cap prevents any single resolution from being more than a 1% drawdown event. Position sizing is the difference between picking up pennies and being run over by the steamroller.
